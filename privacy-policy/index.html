Privacy Policy

GPS Speedometer HUD for iPhone and iPad. Last updated 20 October 2026.

This policy explains what the GPS Speedometer HUD app (the app) does with your data. The app is made by Foobar Creative (we, us), an app studio in Adelaide, Australia. We are bound by the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth). This policy adds to the general Foobar Creative privacy policy. Where the two differ, this policy applies to the app.

In short

  • The app uses your location to show your speed, your heading, the map and nearby speed cameras.
  • Your speed and heading are worked out on your device.
  • We do not sell your data, and we do not use it for advertising.
  • The app uses a small number of third-party services for maps, crash reports, analytics and purchases. They are listed below.

Location

The app asks for permission to use your location. It uses your location to calculate your speed and heading, to centre the map, and to find known speed cameras near you. Your speed and heading are calculated on your device and are not sent to us.

To show speed cameras near you, the app sends the area you are in to our speed camera database, hosted on Google Firebase (Cloud Firestore), and downloads the cameras in that area. We do not keep a history of your location or your trips.

If the app asks for background location and you allow it, the app can keep tracking your speed and checking for nearby speed cameras while it is not on screen. You can change or remove location access at any time in the iOS Settings app, under Privacy & Security, then Location Services.

Maps and place search

The map and the place search in the app use the Google Maps SDK and Google Places. When the map loads or you search for a place, your device sends the map area or your search text to Google. Google handles that data under the Google Privacy Policy.

Speed camera data

Speed camera locations come from OpenStreetMap contributors and are available under the Open Database License. The data describes camera locations, not you.

Analytics and crash reports

The app uses Firebase Analytics and Firebase Crashlytics, both from Google, to help us understand how the app is used and to fix crashes. These services collect an anonymous install identifier, your device model, your iOS version, the app version, your approximate region, in-app events such as which screens are opened, and crash logs. They do not collect your name or your email address.

The app also uses Firebase Authentication to create an anonymous identifier for your installation. This identifier is not linked to your name, your email address or your Apple ID. Google handles this data under the Firebase privacy and security page.

Purchases and subscriptions

Apple processes all payments through the App Store. We never see your card details. The app uses RevenueCat to check whether your installation has an active purchase or subscription. RevenueCat receives an anonymous identifier, your purchase receipts and your subscription status, and handles them under the RevenueCat Privacy Policy.

App configuration

When the app starts, it downloads a small configuration file from ricsantos.net, a server run by the app developer. The file tells the app whether an update is required. The request contains no personal data beyond what any web request includes, such as your IP address.

Children

The app is a driving aid and is not directed at children.

Your rights

You can ask us what data we hold about you, ask us to correct it, or ask us to delete it. Most of the data described above is anonymous, so we may not be able to link it to you. To make a request, contact us using the link below.

Changes to this policy

We may update this policy when the app changes. The date at the top of this page shows the latest version. If a change is significant, we will note it in the app or on the App Store page.

Contact us

If you have a question about this policy or about your data, send us a message.
